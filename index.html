This course introduces students to fundamental concepts and theories within sport and exercise psychology. The goal is to provide a broad overview of major topics within the area of physical activity psychology — covering human cognition, emotion, and behaviour within physical activity contexts.

Knowledge of sport and exercise psychology theory and applications will assist students in understanding underlying psychological processes as they relate to physical activity performance and participation. These skills are relevant to many kinesiology and health-related professional practices.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Describe sport and exercise psychology theories and constructs and discuss their relevance to physical activity contexts.
  • Apply theoretical perspectives and constructs to case studies and provide recommendations for intervention and applied practice.
  • Critically assess sport and exercise psychology theory, constructs, and knowledge application.

In-Person Seminars (×2)
May 20 & June 8 · In class · 5% each
10%

Students are expected to come to class prepared to engage in seminar-style discussions based on lecture material and assigned readings. Discussions will take place in small groups and focus on applying course concepts to sport and physical activity experiences.

Grading (completion-based): Each seminar is worth 5% and requires: (1) attendance confirmed via sign-in sheet, and (2) a Canvas reflection submitted by 11:59 PM on the day of the seminar. Both must be completed to receive marks.

If a seminar is missed due to a documented medical issue or personal emergency, the 5% will be transferred to the final exam, consistent with the academic concession policy.
Applied Reflection
Due Thursday May 28, 11:59 PM PST · Canvas submission · 500–700 words
10%

Students will select one concept or theory from the first three weeks of the course and apply it to a real sport, exercise, or movement experience — either their own, or one they have observed. The paper should demonstrate understanding of the concept, connect it meaningfully to the chosen example, and reflect critically on what the theory explains well and where it may fall short.

Requirements: 500–700 words, written in clear prose, citing at least two sources (one may be the textbook/class notes).

Evaluated on: Conceptual accuracy · Quality of application · Critical reflection · Engagement with course sources

⚠️ AI Use Policy: The use of generative AI is not permitted at any stage of this assignment — including brainstorming, outlining, or drafting. All submissions will be reviewed through Turnitin.

Assessment Policies

Late Assignments

Late assignments are deducted at 10% per day (including weekends). Assignments are not accepted after 6 days. Submit all work via Canvas or in person during lecture — not by email, unless otherwise agreed with the instructor.

Academic Concession

Due dates and exams will not be rescheduled except for a medical issue, personal emergency, or academic conflict. If you miss a midterm or case study exam, the weighting transfers to the final exam. Contact the instructor as soon as possible.

Marking Discussions

Contact the TA no sooner than 24 hours and no later than one week after the assignment is returned. If concerns remain, submit a re-grade request to the instructor within one week of speaking with the TA. Re-graded work may receive a higher, lower, or the same mark.
